Prototype A · Darkroom

Photo-first and quiet. The interface steps back so the pictures carry the colour. Built for culling and rating, with the keyboard first. ← All prototypes

1 · Colour system

Before
Blue-tinted slate surfaces (#0b0f17, #1e293b), a sky-blue accent and glow shadows on selected cards.
After
Hue-free greys from #121212 to #2a2a2a, plus one amber accent used only for picks, selection and the primary action.
Why
Tinted chrome shifts how you perceive a photo's white balance (simultaneous contrast). Lightroom and Capture One use neutral grey for this reason. With one accent, amber always means "this is chosen".

2 · App shell: header → nav rail

Before
AppHeader holds about 13 controls: brand and "Phase 4 Pro" badge, five labelled view tabs, Light, Dim, two panel toggles, Scan, Sync and Export.
After
A 56px icon rail with tooltips and shortcuts holds the views and panel toggles. The top bar keeps only search, a job-status pill, Sync and Import. Export moves to the selection dock, where it applies to something.
Why
Fewer competing targets (Hick's law). The views live on one axis and actions on another. The rail also gives the desktop (Electron) build a native-app feel.

3 · Gallery card → justified rows

Before
PhotoCard is a bordered card cropped to 4:3, with a filename caption, a hover lift and a glow on selection.
After
Borderless tiles in justified rows that keep each photo's aspect ratio. The title and exposure appear in a hover scrim. Selection shows an amber ring, an inset and a check, and a pick shows a badge.
Why
Portraits are no longer cut off, and roughly 30% more photos fit on screen. The API already returns dimensions, so the row layout can be computed before any image loads, and it still works with the TanStack row virtualizer.

4 · Filter sidebar → library panel

Before
Stacked accordion "dropdown cards", emoji labels (📁 ⭐ 🗂️) mixed with lucide icons, and a hotkeys card that takes permanent space.
After
Flat sections with consistent icons. Each camera row has a thin bar showing its share of the library. Active filters reappear as removable chips next to the result count. Shortcuts move into tooltips.
Why
You can see what is filtering the grid without opening the sidebar. The bars put the Analytics information where you make decisions.

5 · EXIF inspector

Before
A histogram drawn from sine waves (it looks the same for every photo), 2×2 boxed exposure tiles with emoji, and rating in its own section.
After
A preview at the top, and a histogram computed from the thumbnail's pixels (try different photos). A one-line monospace strip shows aperture, shutter, ISO and focal length. Rating and Pick share one row with key hints. Details are a definition list with copy-hash.
Why
Honest data: a decorative histogram suggests information that isn't there. In the real app the thumbnail comes from the same origin, so canvas can read its pixels.

6 · Selection dock and lightbox

Before
A dock that is always rendered, and Export JSON in the header acting on "the selection or everything".
After
The dock slides up only when something is selected and shows the count and size, then Pick, Rate, Add to collection, Export and Clear. The lightbox is pure black with an exposure line and arrow-key paging.
Why
Actions sit next to what they act on, and nothing is ambiguous about what will be exported.
