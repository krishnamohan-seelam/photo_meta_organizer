Photo Meta
Library

Prototype B · Atelier

Editorial and warm. The library reads like a photo book: trips become chapters, metadata becomes readable captions, and filters sit in one quiet row. ← All prototypes

1 · Palette and typography

Before
Tailwind-default slate and sky blue, Inter everywhere, uppercase micro-labels on every section, and emoji used as icons.
After
Warm paper (#f5f1ea), ink text and one terracotta accent. Fraunces serif for headings, titles and numbers, Inter for the interface. Dark mode is warm charcoal, not blue-black.
Why
A distinct personality instead of the generic dashboard look. The serif type brings the feel of printed photo books and gallery labels, which suits a personal library.

2 · Navigation and header

Before
Segmented buttons with icons and labels, plus a row of theme, dim, panel, scan, sync and export buttons.
After
Text tabs with an underline indicator (Library, Moments, Places, Collections, Insights), an expanding search pill, a theme toggle and one primary "Add photos" button. Scan and Sync live behind it.
Why
The header answers two questions only: where am I, and what is the main thing I can do?

3 · Filter sidebar → chip bar and a describing sentence

Before
A permanent 280px sidebar of accordion cards (Camera, Tags, Collections, Hotkeys).
After
A sticky row of chips (Camera, Place, Tags, 4★+, Picks, Sort) with popovers and live counts. The headline sentence restates the filter in plain English, for example "6 photos tagged japan, shot on Sony α7 IV".
Why
Photos get the full width. The sentence makes the active filter impossible to miss, which matters because the API ANDs tags together.

4 · Chapters instead of a flat grid

Before
One uniform grid sorted by date. Timeline is a separate view.
After
Photos are grouped into chapters by country and month, built from captured_at and GPS. Each chapter header shows a date range, places, cameras and count, with "Select chapter" on hover. Inside, a mosaic gives the top-rated pick a 2×2 hero tile; portraits span two rows and panoramas two columns.
Why
People remember photos by trip and place, not by filename. The rhythm of the mosaic makes scanning a large library feel lighter.

5 · Photo card

Before
Filename caption (DSC04928_Tokyo_Tower_Sunset.ARW), a border and an always-visible checkbox.
After
A human title derived from the filename ("Tokyo Tower Sunset") and the place, shown on hover. Picks get a terracotta ribbon. The select circle appears on hover or once a selection exists.
Why
Readable at a glance, and the photo stays clean until you interact with it.

6 · Inspector → museum-label detail sheet

Before
A 330px side panel of boxed EXIF tiles and a fake histogram, competing with the grid for space.
After
A large sheet: the image on the left; on the right a label card with place and date, a serif title, and a sentence like "Shot on Canon EOS R5 with RF 24-70mm…". Four large spec numbers, rating and pick, editable tags, a minimap from GPS, and file and hash folded away.
Why
Metadata reads as a story first and as data second. Technical fields stay one click away for power users.
